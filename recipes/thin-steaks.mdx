Thin steaks (about 1/2 inch or 1.25 cm thick or less) cook in just a few minutes. The key is very high heat and a short cook time so the outside browns before the inside overcooks.

## Ingredients

- 2 thin steaks (sirloin, flank, skirt, or thin-cut ribeye)
- 1 tablespoon high smoke point oil (canola, avocado, or vegetable)
- Kosher salt
- Freshly ground black pepper
- Optional: 1 tablespoon butter, 2 garlic cloves (smashed), a sprig of thyme or rosemary

## Equipment

- Cast iron or heavy stainless steel skillet
- Tongs
- Paper towels
- Instant-read thermometer (optional)

## Steps

<Steps>
  <Step title="Prep the steaks">
    Take the steaks out of the fridge 15 to 30 minutes before cooking. Pat them completely dry with paper towels, then season both sides generously with salt and pepper.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Heat the pan">
    Heat the skillet over high heat until it is very hot and just starting to smoke. Add the oil and swirl to coat.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Sear the first side">
    Lay the steaks in the pan away from you. Do not move them. Cook for 1 to 2 minutes until a deep brown crust forms.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Flip and finish">
    Flip the steaks and cook for another 1 to 2 minutes. If using butter, garlic, and herbs, add them now and spoon the melted butter over the steaks.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Rest and slice">
    Move the steaks to a cutting board and rest for 3 to 5 minutes. Slice against the grain and serve.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Doneness guide

| Doneness    | Internal temperature | Approximate time per side |
| ----------- | -------------------- | ------------------------- |
| Rare        | 120 to 125°F (49 to 52°C) | 1 minute             |
| Medium rare | 130 to 135°F (54 to 57°C) | 1 to 1.5 minutes     |
| Medium      | 140 to 145°F (60 to 63°C) | 1.5 to 2 minutes     |

<Note>
  Steak temperature rises a few degrees while resting, so pull it from the pan slightly below your target.
</Note>

## Tips

- **Dry is key.** Surface moisture creates steam and prevents browning.
- **Don't crowd the pan.** Cook in batches if needed so the pan stays hot.
- **Cut against the grain.** This matters most for flank and skirt steak, which can be chewy otherwise.
- **Skip the low heat.** Medium heat gives a gray, overcooked steak before any crust forms.
